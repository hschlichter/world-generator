Eldermark LOD and Streaming

How the Eldermark test world encodes level of detail and streaming in OpenUSD with UsdLod, and what an engine has to implement to draw and stream it. The numbers come from the generated world: a 10 km × 10 km march of 100 regions and 1600 tiles, 31 assets (68 variants) with 8 levels each.

1Terms

cell
A square of the streaming grid. Two cell levels: region (1000 m, 10 × 10 in the world) and tile (250 m, 4 × 4 per region).
LOD root
A prim with LODRootAPI. Every cell and every placed asset is one.
level
One child of a LOD root, a complete representation of it. Index 0 is the finest. A cell's level 0 is called full.
domain
The lod:domain of a heuristic: imaging (what is drawn), streaming (what is resident), physics (what collides).
resident
A level whose content is composed: a payload level after it is loaded, a referenced level always.
drawn
The level the engine renders for a root this frame. It is resident by construction.
ring
The band of distances in which one level of a cell kind is drawn, for example tile medium from 220 to 420 m.
distance
Always the distance from the camera position to the root's bounding box (0 inside it), never to its centre.

2World and coordinates

10 km × 10 km, centred on the origin, upAxis = Z, metersPerUnit = 1, x east, y north. Region R_i_j spans x from −5000 + 1000·i and y from −5000 + 1000·j. Tile T_a_b is the a-th column and b-th row inside its region. Every cell prim carries a translate to its centre, and payload content is in cell-local coordinates, so points stay within ±500 m (regions) or ±125 m (tiles) and are exact in single precision.

3Hierarchy and files

The world is a two-level grid of LOD roots. A region's full level contains its 16 tile roots. A tile's full level contains 25 ground-chunk roots (0.5 m and 1 m terrain), every placed asset and the ground cover. Every other level is reduced terrain plus one PointInstancer of the assets' own LOD levels.

4USD authoring contract

These rules hold for every LOD root in the world. validate checks each of them, so an engine can rely on them.

  • MUST A root's levels are exactly its active, defined children, in order, finest first. Nothing else is a child of a LOD root.
  • MUST lod:default:index is the coarsest level. Every other level is authored visibility = invisible. A consumer that ignores UsdLod therefore draws the cheapest complete world: 100 region proxies: 2.1 M terrain triangles plus 0.83 M instanced lod7 assets (31.6 M triangles).
  • MUST Heuristics and their bounds live in a sibling LODHeuristics/<cell> Xform with the cell's transform, never inside the root. They are composed while every level is unloaded, which is when a streamer needs them.
  • MUST Every thresholds array has levels − 1 values. Distance thresholds ascend, screen-size thresholds descend. Blend thresholds are empty or have the same count.
  • MUST Each heuristic's bounding volume (a guide-purpose box mesh, plus center or extent) contains the content of every level of its root.
  • MUST Per level boundary: imaging distance threshold ≤ streaming load radius ≤ streaming unload radius.
  • MUST A child cell's thresholds lie inside its parent's first boundary. Tile imaging (≤ 420 m) is below region imaging (600 m), and tile unload (≤ 580 m) is below region load (700 m).
  • MAY The full level of a non-finest cell is a payload or a reference (full_arc). In this world it is a reference.

        uniform float[] thresholds = [0.7357, 0.3011]
        uniform float[] blendThresholds = [0.6255, 0.262]
    }
    def LODDistanceHeuristic "physics_distance" {
        uniform token lod:domain = "physics"
        uniform float[] thresholds = [120, 420]
    }
    def LODDistanceHeuristic "streaming_distance" {
        uniform token lod:domain = "streaming"
        uniform float[] thresholds = [300, 500]                    # load radius per boundary
        custom uniform float[] streaming:unloadThresholds = [360, 580]  # unload radius per boundary
        rel boundingVolume = <../bounds>
    }
}

UsdLod defines the imaging, physics and audio domains. streaming is a custom domain token, and streaming:unloadThresholds is a custom attribute; UsdLod has no notion of residency or hysteresis. Both pass usdchecker and the UsdValidation validators.

Imaging, distance form

The LOD value of a root at distance d is a sum over its boundaries i with threshold ti and blend threshold bi:

value(d) = Σi  { 0                         if d < ti
           { min(1, (d − ti) / (bi − ti))   if d ≥ ti   (1 when there is no blend band)

drawn level   = floor(value + 0.5)                       # an engine that does not cross-fade
cross-fade    = levels floor(value) and ceil(value), weight frac(value)

This is what UsdLod.DistanceHeuristicQuery.ComputeLOD returns, and what the manifest's expected levels are computed with. Without cross-fading, a tile switches full → medium at 235 m and medium → simplified at 440 m; a region switches at 640 m and 1250 m.

Imaging, screen-size form

For engines that prefer projected size, every cell also has a projectedSphere screen-size heuristic. Its thresholds are calibrated so the switch happens at the distance thresholds for the reference camera (36 mm aperture, 24 mm focal length, 16:9). Values above 1 are normal: they mean that the sphere around a 1 km cell covers more than the view. Assets have screen-size heuristics only.

The screen-size thresholds are calibrated on one representative cell extent, and a projected sphere grows with a cell's height range. So with the screen-size form, switch distances vary from cell to cell. At the validation viewpoints it picks the same cell level as the distance form 56–89 % of the time. The distance form is the reference.

Streaming

The streaming heuristic has the same shape as the imaging distance heuristic. Its thresholds are the load radii, and streaming:unloadThresholds are the matching unload radii. §7 defines how they are used.

6Selecting what to draw

Every frame, walk from the regions down. A root's children are only visited when the root draws full. A level that is not resident is never drawn: the root falls back to the nearest coarser resident level.

def select_drawn(eye):
    queue = region_roots()
    while queue:
        root   = queue.pop()
        value  = imaging_heuristic(root).ComputeLOD(distance_to_box(eye, root))
        want   = round_half_up(value)                  # or cross-fade floor/ceil
        drawn  = first(i for i in want .. last if resident(root.level[i]))
        if drawn != want:  stats.late += 1            # content was not ready: coarser stand-in
        show(root.level[drawn]); hide(other levels)
        if drawn == 0:  queue += child_roots(root.level[0])   # tiles, or asset roots in a tile

Reference: Selector.select(frustum, load=False) in lodselect.py. The usdview plugin applies the result as session-layer visibility, because Storm does not implement UsdLod.

7Streaming: what to keep resident

Residency is decided separately from drawing, by the streaming domain. Per root, the streamer keeps a state s, the finest level that must be resident. The root's level s and every coarser level stay resident, so the fallback chain in §6 always has an answer.

def update_residency(eye):
    wanted = {}
    queue  = region_roots()
    while queue:
        root  = queue.pop()
        d     = distance_to_box(eye, root)
        s_load   = count(t <= d for t in load_radii(root))        # streaming thresholds
        s_unload = count(u <= d for u in unload_radii(root))      # streaming:unloadThresholds
        prev  = state.get(root)
        s     = s_load if prev is None or s_load <= prev else min(s_load, max(prev, s_unload))
        state[root] = s                                         # hysteresis: finer at once, coarser late
        wanted |= payload_levels(root, s .. last)               # s and every coarser level
        if s == 0:  queue += child_roots(root.level[0])       # children only while full is wanted
    forget state of roots not visited
    request(wanted − resident);  release(resident − wanted)    # cancel in-flight requests that became unwanted

Choosing the radii

The load radius is the imaging switch distance plus the prefetch lead: lead time × camera speed. With the current values, a tile's full level is requested 65 m before it is drawn, which is 2.2 s at 30 m/s. The replay in §11 shows a median lead of 2.3 s and a minimum of 0.2 s at 50 MB/s. The unload radius sets how far the camera can back off without a reload. 60–80 m covers most wandering along an edge.

Request order

The reference streamer issues requests in path order on one channel. An engine MAY prioritise: coarser levels before finer ones (they are the fallback), then nearer roots first. The measurements in §11 use the simple order, so they are a conservative baseline.

8Asset LOD and instancing

Assets live only inside tile full levels, so they are streamed with their tile. Each placement is an instanceable prim referencing the asset's interface file with a variant selection. Each asset × variant becomes one prototype, which contains the asset's LOD root (Geom) with 8 levels. The default is lod7.

  • Asset heuristics are projectedSphere screen-size heuristics with an authored extent per variant. Each threshold step is about 2× in projected size, so each switch removes about half the detail. The blend band is the upper 15 % of each step.
  • An engine evaluates asset roots per instance, because the selected level depends on the instance's transform, while the prototype stays shared. usdview can only do this by de-instancing a placement that needs a non-default level.
  • A tile is drawn full within 220–250 m of its box, so its assets can be up to about 575 m from the camera. That is why the coarse asset levels (switching at 200–770 m for large assets) are used.
  • Buildings ([lod.assets.building]) have 2.5× smaller screen thresholds than other structures, about 1.6× the switch distance.
  • Coarse cell levels instance the asset levels. Every coarse level holds one PointInstancer whose prototypes reference <asset>_geom.usdc</<asset>_geom/<variant>/lod<i>> and bind the asset's materials. Each instance carries the level its asset would select at the distance where that coarse level takes over (switch distances × instance scale), so a cell switch keeps every tree's shape: trees are lod5–6 in tile medium, lod7 in the region levels. Size filters per level drop small assets (bushes, small rocks) further out; region proxy keeps trees over 5 m. Prototypes author collision off.
  • Ground cover uses PointInstancers, 4 chunks per tile, with no LOD. It is only drawn inside tile full.

9Terrain, seams and materials

  • Every terrain mesh samples one global 1 m heightfield: ground chunk base at 1 m, tile medium at 2 m, tile simplified and region medium at 5 m, region proxy at 10 m. Neighbours at the same level share bit-identical edge vertices and normals.
  • Ground chunks. A tile's full level has 25 chunk roots of 50 m (children of the level, like tiles inside a region's full), with customData.ground = true. fine (drawn within 50 m, blend to 58 m; loaded from 100 m, released beyond 120 m) is a 0.5 m mesh: Catmull-Rom interpolation of the heightfield plus synthesized micro-relief (tussocks, forest-floor hummocks, ridged rock, ridge-and-furrow on the farm strips, smoothed paths and settlements), on a per-tile 1024² texture (0.24 m/texel) sampled from the terrain source textures. It is a payload into one prim of the tile's _ground.usdc, so one file per tile serves 25 payloads; the fine level prim carries customData.bytes (its geometry size) for streaming budgets. base is the 1 m mesh inline in the tile's full level, the default, and the collision level.
  • MUST NOT stitch. Different levels can sit side by side (a tile at full next to one at medium, or a region's tiles next to a neighbouring region's medium). Every terrain mesh has a vertical skirt deep enough to cover the worst gap against any level that can neighbour it. Validation measures the real gaps at every pair of levels.
  • Tile levels and region medium use the terrain material: the region's 2048² baked colour plus a tiled detail normal. Region proxy uses terrain_far: a 1024² colour and a macro normal map baked against the proxy's 10 m grid. So the 600 m switch (tiles to region medium) changes geometry density but not shading.
  • Materials are UsdPreviewSurface, with texture inputs overridden per cell through Material interface inputs.

10Physics

  • Tiles have a physics distance heuristic: 1 m terrain within 120 m, 2 m terrain to 420 m, 5 m beyond. Physics needs those levels resident too. The streaming radii already cover it, because physics thresholds are not larger than the imaging ones.
  • Every collider (a mesh with PhysicsCollisionAPI) authors physics:collisionEnabled explicitly: true on its root's default level, false on every other level. Nothing relies on the schema fallback. A consumer that ignores UsdLod therefore collides with exactly the default levels: the chunk base meshes inside a loaded tile full, tile simplified and region proxy terrain, and asset lod7. Point-instanced stand-ins in coarse levels are always false. A physics-aware consumer moves the true to the level its physics heuristic selects, the same way the renderer handles visibility.
  • Rocks and structures have physics heuristics that select decimated levels (rocks lod3 near, structures lod4 near, lod7 hulls far). Render lod0 is never used for collision.

Reading these numbers:

  • With draw-only, every switch to a finer level is late, because the request starts when the level is needed. Streaming removes almost all late draws and cuts reloads by more than 80 % (1157 to 200).
  • The replay composes a full level once it is resident, so the ground chunks inside a tile's full level take part. Their fine levels are requested only after the tile's full level has landed, which makes them the last link in the chain: every remaining late draw at 50 MB/s is a fine ground chunk, briefly drawn with its 1 m base.
  • The cost is memory: about 1.5× the resident set, made up of prefetched finer levels plus the coarser fallback levels.
  • At 30 m/s the current radii need about 20 MB/s of sustained read bandwidth: at 20 MB/s 8 roots are briefly late (1.2 root-seconds in a 15-minute flight). Below that, late draws grow; at 5 MB/s the fine ground (about 600 MB over the flight) saturates the channel. Late draws fall back to a coarser level and never leave a hole.
  • Late draws count each root separately, so one late region switch shows up as several tile events.

Rendering budget for reference: the far field (100 region proxies) is 2.1 M terrain triangles plus 31.6 M instanced triangles (0.83 M lod7 instances). A region medium is 82k terrain plus about 0.87 M instanced triangles. One tile full is 135k terrain triangles (1 m base) plus about 0.82 M instanced triangles at the asset default level (3.0k instances, 70k ground-cover points); a fine ground chunk is 21k triangles.

12Engine checklist

  1. Open root.usda with payloads unloaded. All cell roots and their heuristics are composed: 100 regions and 1600 tiles, 7.4 MB of structure (root.usda 430 KB plus 100 region full files of 70 KB). The 25 ground chunk roots of a tile appear when its full level is loaded.
  2. For every root, read its levels (children, in order) and its heuristics by domain from lod:heuristics.
  3. Each frame: update residency (§7) from the camera, issue asynchronous loads and releases, and cancel unwanted requests.
  4. Each frame: select drawn levels (§6), falling back to coarser resident levels. Optionally cross-fade within blend bands.
  5. Own visibility: show the drawn level and hide the others, overriding the authored defaults.
  6. Inside drawn tile full levels, walk into the ground chunk roots like any nested cell, and select asset levels per instance with the screen-size heuristics.
  7. Physics: select collision levels with the physics heuristics, and keep them resident.
  8. Don't stitch terrain: skirts cover every level combination.
  9. Measure late draws, lead time and reloads. Compare with §11 and world/stream_replay.json.
